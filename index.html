    Attendus du dossier (aide-mémoire correcteur)

    Facts to spot

    • A heatwave leaves some 25 million people short of water A l.10-11
    • Delhi ranks second among the world's most water-stressed cities (NITI Aayog, 2019) B l.1-3
    • Industrial waste pollutes the Yamuna river B l.5-6
    • Kusumpur Pahari: over 50,000 residents, no piped water, labelled an "illegal settlement" after three generations A l.21-26
    • Rationing: water once a day instead of twice in South Delhi B l.11-13
    • Rs 2,000 fines and a crackdown on water misuse by the DJB B l.16-21
    • Political rows between states and an appeal to the Supreme Court A l.13-17; B l.24-28
    • Crowds, jerrycans and mostly women queuing at a water tanker C

    Implicit meaning

    • Water exposes social inequality: a slum wedged between glittering malls and upmarket enclaves, its residents working for the wealthy
    • A failure of governance: political rivalries between neighbouring states
    • Latent tension and violence ("People can kill you for water" A l.30)
    • Climate change aggravates a structural problem
    • Measures are reactive and come too late B l.21-22
    • "Illegal" status amounts to a denial of rights; the burden of fetching water falls on women C

    Specificities and possible outline

    • A: narrative report from The New York Times, an American outlook with a human focus
    • B: Indian business daily, explanatory and solution-oriented
    • C: Reuters photograph, visual testimony with tight framing
    • I. A crisis with multiple causes (climate, demand, pollution)
    • II. A population unequally affected (the poor, daily struggle, tensions)
    • III. Authorities facing governance challenges (rationing, fines, inter-state cooperation)
